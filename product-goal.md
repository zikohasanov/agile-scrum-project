# Product Goal and Working Agreements

## Product goal

Enable customers at one fictional restaurant to submit a clear pickup request and let staff make an explicit fulfilment decision, while reducing avoidable re-entry and status-check effort during a measured pilot.

## Product boundaries

First release: public menu, cart, pickup request, staff acceptance/rejection, controlled order states, and secure status lookup. Excluded: delivery, POS integration, loyalty, scheduled orders, and online payment.

## Roles in this simulation

- **Product owner (simulated):** fictional restaurant owner role; accountable for value and ordering the backlog.
- **Developers (simulated):** cross-functional delivery group; responsible for a usable increment and quality.
- **Business analyst (portfolio author role):** clarifies outcomes, rules, examples, dependencies, and traceability in support of the team. This is a role-play, not a real job assignment.

## Working agreements

- Refinement identifies an outcome, testable acceptance criteria, dependencies, and unresolved policy questions.
- Sprint planning selects work only after discussing capacity and a coherent sprint goal.
- Daily Scrum is a Developers' planning conversation, not a status report to a manager.
- Review includes a usable increment and stakeholder feedback; a demonstration alone does not imply release approval.
- Retrospective selects one actionable experiment and an owner.
- Definition of Done includes acceptance evidence, accessibility review, privacy/security review when relevant, and updated support guidance.

## Planning assumptions

Two one-week sprints are used solely to make the example concrete. A real duration, team size, and capacity would be agreed by the team. No velocity, throughput, or delivery date is claimed.
